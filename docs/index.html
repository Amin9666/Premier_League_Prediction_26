01Why this, and not a bolt-on

The Premier League predictor this is built on estimates things like "P(Arsenal finish top 4)" by simulating 10,000 full seasons and counting outcomes — classical Monte Carlo. That's not incidental: Monte Carlo estimation is exactly the problem Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) was built to speed up, and it's the same technique behind quantum finance's Monte Carlo option-pricing work (Stamatopoulos et al., 2020; IBM/Goldman Sachs).

Classical sampling drives estimation error down as 1/√M for M samples. Quantum amplitude estimation drives the same error down as 1/M — a quadratic reduction in how many queries you need for the same precision.

02What's being estimated

Both algorithms below estimate the same target: p = 0.4584, Arsenal's fitted home-win probability against Man City from this season's actual, backtested Dixon-Coles model — not a synthetic textbook value.

The probability is loaded onto one qubit as an amplitude, Ry(θ)|0⟩ = √(1−p)|0⟩ + √p|1⟩, then re-estimated by measurement. That amplitude-loading step is classical — see the caveats below for exactly what that does and doesn't mean.

04What's real here, and what isn't

Read before citing this anywhere

  • The probability is real. 0.4584 comes from the same Dixon-Coles model backtested across 3 Premier League seasons elsewhere in this project — not a toy number picked to make a clean chart.
  • The amplitude is loaded, not computed. A single Ry rotation encodes a classically pre-computed p. The circuit does not derive p from first principles (that would mean quantum state preparation of the underlying Poisson goal distributions — a genuinely harder problem, not attempted here). What's being tested is the estimation algorithm's query complexity, not a claim that goals were simulated quantum-mechanically.
  • Simulator only. Everything above runs on Qiskit Aer's noiseless statevector simulator. Real NISQ hardware today doesn't hold coherence long enough for amplitude estimation's Grover depth to realize this speedup in practice — this demonstrates the algorithmic principle, not a claim of practical advantage today.
  • Canonical (QPE-based) amplitude estimation was tried too and showed the same directional result, but with a stepped, less legible error curve — a known consequence of its fixed 2k-point measurement grid. Iterative Amplitude Estimation (shown above) is the adaptive variant actually used in the quantum finance literature this is modeled on, and is why it's the one shown here.
